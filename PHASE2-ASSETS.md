## The before/after finding

**There is exactly one genuine before/after project in the whole set, and it is
told across eight photographs.**

BEFORE: p22, p09, p06, p23 → AFTER: p15, p16, p02, p14

Confirmed the same garden by six matching features across the two groups: the
purple-leaf prunus, the red-flowered camellia, the bluebells, the cherry blossom,
the white-rendered neighbouring house, and the mossy close-board boundary fence.

It also matches a review word for word. James Ward, two years ago:

> "From laying paving slabs, building numerous sleeper walls, to re-leveling the
> whole garden with an original gradient of 10ft, his workmanship & skill set
> enabled him to complete the full project. The before & after photos are a
> testament to Ty's hard work"

p29 is a before with no matching after. No other pair exists.

### This kills the slider

**No before/after pair shares a camera position.** Different spots, different days,
different focal lengths. A drag slider makes an implicit promise that the two frames
are the same view, and when they are not it reads as a trick. For a business whose
entire pitch is honest proof of work, a slider that looks like it is hiding something
is the worst possible mechanic.

The mechanic has to be one that treats before and after as a *sequence* rather than
a *registration*. Recommendation carried into Phase 3: a scroll-driven sequence that
holds the four before frames, then transitions to the four after frames, with the
gradient itself as the narrative spine. Detail in the Phase 3 plan.

## Photographic characteristics to design around

- **Two cameras, two eras.** p04, p07, p13, p17, p26 are older, softer, lower
  contrast (roughly 1600x1195, an older phone). Everything in Tier 1 is modern
  iPhone, sharp, wide. Do not mix the two in the same row.
- **Weather is split.** p02, p14, p15, p16 are hard blue-sky. p10, p12, p20, p24,
  p28, p31 are flat British overcast. The overcast frames need lift; the blue-sky
  frames need the sky pulled back so they do not shout.
- **Sky is the problem in every landscape frame.** 4:3 means roughly the top third
  is white or blue sky in most shots. Any full-bleed hero has to crop from the
  bottom of the sky, not the top of the ground.
- **Clutter to crop out:** a wheelbarrow and truck (p01), a Tango can (p24), tool
  bags (p20), building debris and mud (p31, p20), a palm trunk dominating the left
  edge (p21), someone's arm and leg at the frame edges (p05).
- **No image is currently hero-shaped.** Everything is 4:3. A 16:9 or wider hero
  needs a deliberate crop, and p15 and p16 are the only two that survive it.
